Privacy policy

GeoGym is a free geography game with no accounts and no sign-up. This page explains what information is used when you play, and why.

Last updated: November 18, 2026

What we don't collect

GeoGym has no accounts, logins or forms. We don't ask for your email address or any other personal information to play. The only exception is the name you choose to type in for Multiplayer, described below.

Your progress stays on your device

Your progress, best times, Daily Challenge results and settings (like sounds and answer style) are saved in your browser's local storage, on your own device. They are not sent to us. To delete them, clear this site's data in your browser settings. This also resets your progress.

Hosting

GeoGym is hosted by Cloudflare. Like any website host, Cloudflare receives technical information when you visit, such as your IP address and browser type, to deliver the site and protect it from attacks. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Multiplayer

When you play Multiplayer, your browser connects to our game server (run on Cloudflare) so both players can see each other's progress. The server receives the name you type in (it can be anything; you don't have to use your real name), the game code, which quiz you picked, and your progress and score in that game. Your opponent sees your name and score. This information is used only to run the game and is deleted about an hour after both players leave. Your name is also saved in your browser so you don't have to type it again.

Daily Challenge leaderboard

When you finish the Daily Challenge, your score and time are added to that day's public leaderboard on our game server (run on Cloudflare). On the leaderboard you appear only under a made-up name such as "Swift Falcon", picked at random from a fixed list; you can't type or choose your own. To recognise your score, your browser keeps a random player ID and sends it with your score. The leaderboard stores that ID, the made-up name, your score and your time, and deletes each day's board a few days later.

Photos from Wikipedia

Photos of capitals and landmarks are loaded directly from Wikipedia (Wikimedia Foundation) by your browser, so Wikimedia receives the request, including your IP address. See the Wikimedia privacy policy.

Advertising

GeoGym may show ads to keep the site free. Ads are provided by Google (Google AdSense).

  • Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on your prior visits to this website or other websites.
  • Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to you based on your visits to this site and/or other sites on the Internet.
  • You can opt out of personalized advertising by visiting Google Ads Settings. You can also opt out of some third-party vendors' use of cookies for personalized advertising at www.aboutads.info.
  • To learn more, see how Google uses information from sites that use its services.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, you will be asked for your consent before cookies are used for personalized ads, and you can change your choice at any time.

Sharing your results

When you tap Share or Copy result, the picture and link are created on your device and passed to the app you choose. Nothing is sent to us.

Children

GeoGym is made for a general audience, including students. Because there are no accounts or forms, we don't knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

Emailing us

If you email us, we use your email address and message only to reply to you.

Changes to this policy

If we change how GeoGym uses information, we'll update this page and the date at the top.

Contact

Questions about privacy? Email support@geogym.party.
